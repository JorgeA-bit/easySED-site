What is Seafarers' Earnings Deduction (SED)?

5 min read Based on official HMRC guidance

If you work at sea and spend most of your time outside the UK, you could legally pay zero tax on your seafaring income. That's what Seafarers' Earnings Deduction — or SED — is all about. Here's everything you need to know, in plain English.

What is SED in plain English?

Seafarers' Earnings Deduction is a UK tax relief that allows eligible seafarers to deduct 100% of their seafaring earnings from their taxable income. In practice, this means you pay no income tax on the money you earn while working at sea — as long as you qualify.

It's one of the most generous tax reliefs available to any group of workers in the UK. A seafarer earning £50,000 a year who qualifies for SED could save over £10,000 in tax annually.

Important note
SED is a deduction, not an exemption. You still need to file a Self Assessment tax return to claim it — it doesn't apply automatically. easySED helps you build the evidence you need to make that claim confidently.

Who qualifies for SED?

To claim SED, you must meet all three of the following conditions:

  1. You work on a ship. Either all of your duties are performed on a ship, or most of them are — and any duties performed off the ship are directly connected to your work on it.
  2. You spend enough time outside the UK. You need an "eligible period" of at least 365 days, made up mainly of days when you were outside the UK.
  3. You are a UK resident — or a tax resident of an EEA country other than the UK.

What counts as working "on a ship"?

The word "ship" isn't defined in tax law, but HMRC is clear about what doesn't count. If you work on an offshore installation — such as an oil rig or production platform — your vessel is not considered a ship for SED purposes, no matter where in the world it is located.

Offshore installations that do not qualify include:

  • Fixed and floating production platforms
  • Floating storage units
  • Floating production storage and offloading (FPSO) vessels
  • Mobile offshore drilling units — including drillships, semi-submersibles and jack-ups
  • Flotels (accommodation vessels attached to offshore installations)
Offshore workers
This exclusion applies even when an offshore installation is temporarily moving between locations, has changed its use, or has been taken out of service. If the structure exploits mineral resources via a well, it does not qualify — full stop.

Your employment duties count as being performed "outside the UK" if they are carried out on a vessel engaged in a voyage that begins or ends outside the UK. Note: the UK sector of the North Sea is treated as part of the UK for this purpose.

Who cannot claim SED?

✓ You can claim if you are...

  • A UK-resident seafarer on a qualifying ship
  • An EEA-resident seafarer (non-UK) on a qualifying ship
  • A Royal Fleet Auxiliary employee who meets all other conditions
  • A seafarer with more than one job (you can claim for the seafaring job only)

✗ You cannot claim if you are...

  • A Crown employee (e.g. Royal Navy)
  • Not a UK resident or EEA state resident
  • Working on an offshore installation
  • Not spending enough days outside the UK

What is the 365-day eligible period?

To claim SED, you need to build up an "eligible period" of at least 365 consecutive days. This period must be mainly made up of days when you were outside the UK.

You are counted as outside the UK on any day where you are outside the UK at midnight at the end of that day. This is called the midnight rule — and it's the foundation of how SED is calculated. Days off spent abroad also count.

Visits back to the UK during an eligible period are allowed, but with two strict conditions:

  • No single return visit to the UK can last more than 183 consecutive days
  • The total number of days spent in the UK must not exceed half of the total days in your eligible period
Common mistake
You cannot count UK days that happen after your last period abroad. For example: 183 days abroad, followed by 182 days in the UK does not create a valid eligible period — even though it adds up to 365 days. The period must end on a day abroad.

How do you actually claim SED?

If you're a UK resident

You need to register for Self Assessment and complete a tax return for the year you're claiming. Use the HS205 working sheet (available from HMRC) to calculate your eligible period and the amount of the deduction, then enter the figure in box 11 of the Additional Information pages.

In the "Any other information" box on your return, include the names of the ships on which you carried out your duties.

If you're an EEA resident (not UK)

You can't use a standard Self Assessment return. Instead, submit form R43M(SED) — the Repayment Claim form for EEA resident merchant seafarers — to HMRC to reclaim any tax deducted under PAYE.

What is an NT tax code — and should you apply for one?

If you're a UK resident on PAYE, you may be able to ask HMRC for a tax code NT (No Tax). This allows your employer to pay your wages without deducting any income tax in the first place — rather than you paying tax upfront and claiming it back later.

To qualify for an NT code you must:

  • Be a UK resident in a PAYE employment
  • Work on a vessel that qualifies as a ship for SED purposes
  • File a Self Assessment tax return every year
  • Have a contract of at least 12 months — or have been in continuous employment with the same employer for at least 6 months working outside the UK
Important
HMRC will not issue an NT code until after they have processed your first successful SED claim. If your employment changes and you no longer qualify, you must tell HMRC immediately. If it later turns out you weren't entitled to the code, you'll owe all the underpaid tax back in one lump sum. Use form R44 to request code NT.

What records do you need to keep?

HMRC may ask to check your claim at any time. You don't send these documents with your tax return, but you must be able to produce them on request. Keep all of the following:

Completed HS205 working sheet
Air tickets and travel vouchers
Seafarer's discharge book
Passport and visas
Hotel bills and receipts
Freeboard logs of ships you worked on
HMRC can contact your employer
HMRC may reach out to your employer directly to confirm details of your ship's voyage and crew. Make sure the details in your claim match your employment records.

Time limits for claiming

You can't claim SED indefinitely — there are strict deadlines. The general rule is that you have 4 years from the end of the tax year to make a claim. Miss the deadline and the claim is lost.
